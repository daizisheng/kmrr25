\section{Introduction}\label{I:sec:intro}

A well-known theorem of Moreira, Richter and Robertson~\cite{moreira2019proof}, answering a question of
Erd\H{o}s, says that every set $A\subset\N$ of positive upper Banach density contains a sumset
$B+C=\{b+c:b\in B,\ c\in C\}$ with $B$ and $C$ infinite. In the survey~\cite{kra2023problems},
Kra, Moreira, Richter and Robertson collected a large number of open problems around this theorem:
which other infinite configurations must occur in sets of positive density, which density
hypotheses are needed, and what happens in the primes, in random sets, in multiplicative settings and
in other groups.

In this paper we treat twenty of the questions and conjectures posed in~\cite{kra2023problems}
(nineteen questions and Conjecture~4.5); for two of them the answer follows directly from known
results, as explained below. Our main result
concerns sums of more than two infinite sets in abelian groups.

\begin{mainthm}
Let $\Gamma$ be a countably infinite abelian group and let $k\ge1$. Every set $A\subset\Gamma$ of
positive upper Banach density contains a sumset $B_1+\dots+B_k$, where $B_1,\dots,B_k\subset\Gamma$
are infinite.
\end{mainthm}

This is \cref{K:thm:main}; it answers Question~5.14 of~\cite{kra2023problems}. The proof uses the
Kronecker factor, but no higher-order Host--Kra structure theory, nilsystems of step greater than
one, or nilspaces: it extends the short proof of the case $k=2$
by Kra, Moreira, Richter and Robertson~\cite{kra2026short} to an arbitrary number of summands,
building the required configuration one level at a time and keeping track only of the supports of
suitable measures on the Kronecker factor.

The other answers are listed in \cref{I:tab:results}, in the order in which they appear in the
paper. They are of very different depth. Several are short, and some rely in an essential way on
recent work of other authors: Question~5.16 on a theorem of Charamaras and
Mountakis~\cite{charamaras2024finding}, the positive part of Question~\S3.3(a) on a theorem of
Kousek~\cite{kousek2025asymmetric}, Questions~3.31 and~\S3.6(a) on the dynamical part of the density
finite sums theorem of Kra, Moreira, Richter and Robertson~\cite{kra2025density}, and Conjecture~4.5
on a theorem of Tao and Ziegler~\cite{tao2023infinite}. The negative answer to Question~\S3.3(e)
follows directly from known counterexamples (one from~\cite{kra2023problems} itself and one due to
Ackelsberg~\cite{ackelsberg2024counterexamples}), and Question~3.24 is already answered by an example
of Kousek and Radi\'c~\cite{kousek2024infinite}, as we check; we record these for completeness. In
each case we say precisely what is new.

In addition, in \cref{KR:sec:main} we answer a question of Kousek and
Radi\'c~\cite{kousek2024infinite} that is not in~\cite{kra2023problems}: the supremum of the lower
Banach densities of sets $A\subset\N$ such that $B+B+t\not\subset A$ for every infinite $B\subset\N$
and every $t\ge0$ is $1/2$, and the supremum for sets such that $B+B\not\subset A$ for every
infinite $B$ is $3/4$.

\subsection*{Conventions}
We use the numbering of the second arXiv version of~\cite{kra2023problems}. Some questions in the
survey are posed in the running text without a number. We refer to them by the subsection in which
they appear followed by a letter, in order of appearance: for instance, Question~\S3.2(a) is the
first unnumbered question in Subsection~3.2 of~\cite{kra2023problems}. Before answering each
question we restate it in our notation.

Throughout, $\N=\{1,2,3,\dots\}$, $\P$ denotes the set of primes, and for $A\subset\N$
\[
\udens(A)=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{|A\cap[N]|}{N},\qquad
\ubd(A)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sup_{M\ge0}\frac{|A\cap[M+1,M+N]|}{N},
\]
\[
\udlog(A)=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log N}\sum_{n\in A\cap[N]}\frac1n,
\]
where $[N]=\{1,\dots,N\}$. The lower densities $\ldens$ and $\underline{\mathsf d}_{\log}$ are defined
with $\liminf$ in place of $\limsup$, and the natural and logarithmic densities $\dens$ and $\dlog$
are the common values when upper and lower densities agree. The lower Banach density is defined in
\cref{KR:sec:main}. For a \Fol{} sequence $\Phi=(\Phi_N)$ in a countable abelian group we write
$\udens_\Phi(A)=\limsup_N|A\cap\Phi_N|/|\Phi_N|$, and the upper Banach density $\ubd(A)$ is the
supremum of $\udens_\Phi(A)$ over all \Fol{} sequences $\Phi$.
A set is \emph{thick} if it contains arbitrarily long intervals (in an abelian group: translates of
every finite set). Conventions for nonabelian groups are given where they are used. For sets $B,C$ we write $B+C=\{b+c:b\in B,c\in C\}$, $B+t=\{b+t:b\in B\}$,
$mB=\{mb:b\in B\}$ and $B\oplus B=\{b+b':b,b'\in B,\ b\ne b'\}$; thus $B+B$ is the unrestricted
sumset, which contains the elements $2b$.

\subsection*{Questions whose answer depends on the formulation}
For several questions the answer we give concerns the question exactly as it is stated in the survey,
and the literal statement differs from what the authors of the survey probably intended. We include
these answers because the difference is not always obvious and the literal statements have been
repeated in the literature, but in each case we point out the issue explicitly, give the natural
corrected formulation, and say whether it remains open. This applies to
Questions~5.23 and~\S5.4(b) (where the fibre conditions constrain only one coordinate),
Question~\S3.2(a) (where the density condition can be read in two ways; we answer both readings),
Question~3.20 (where $m=1$ is coprime to every $q$; we also answer the question for $m\ge2$),
Question~3.23 (where $B+mB$ contains the diagonal terms $(m+1)b$) and Question~5.6 (where the
sign of $x$ gives an obstruction at the real place).

\subsection*{Formalization}
The main theorem and most of the other results have been formalized in Lean~4 with Mathlib; the
formalization is available at \url{https://github.com/daizisheng/kmrr25}, in the directory
\nolinkurl{lean/Kmrr25}. No file contains \texttt{sorry} or declares an axiom; every main theorem
depends only on the standard axioms of Lean. Where one of our proofs relies on a published theorem
that is not formalized, that theorem is stated as a proposition and appears as an explicit
hypothesis of the formal statement, so that what is formalized is the deduction from it. \Cref{I:tab:lean} gives an overview, and
after each formalized result a paragraph headed \emph{Formalization} names the file and the
main formal statements and says what is not covered.

\subsection*{Organisation}
The paper has three parts, organised by the setting. \Cref{K:sec:main}, at the beginning of the first
part, contains the proof of the main theorem; the other sections of that part concern sumsets in
abelian groups and in $\N^2$. The second part concerns sumsets in the integers: density thresholds
for $B+B$ and $B+B+t$ (\cref{IA:sec:u32a,KR:sec:main}), polynomial and dilated sumsets
(\cref{IA:sec:ordered,IA:sec:BmB}), density Ramsey families (\cref{IB:sec:q331}), sparse and random
sets (\cref{IB:sec:q413,IB:sec:u44a}) and the primes (\cref{IB:sec:c45}). The third part concerns
configurations that mix sums and products. Each section begins by restating the questions it
answers, and the sections can be read independently.
